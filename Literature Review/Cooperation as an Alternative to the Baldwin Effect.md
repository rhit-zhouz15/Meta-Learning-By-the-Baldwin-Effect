- Runs similar experiment to original Baldwin Effect paper (Hinton, Nowlan 1987) without the ? state and simulates 1,024 agents, each with 20 genes of 1 or 0 values [3]
- Focuses on group cooperation instead of individual learning [3]
- Agents partitioned into groups of 4, and the group is fit if at least one agent has a 1 gene for each of the 20 genes [3]
- Instead of discarding agents when unfit, the unfit groups are shuffled and regrouped at every time step across 20 iterations [3]
- Breeding occurrs after 20 time steps and those agents that achieve fitness earlier are 20 times more likely to reproduce than agents in groups that never became fit [3]
- Parents are selected in fit groups by random so selection is initially blind [3]
- Without cooperation, agents averaged 6 genes with a 1 across the population [3]
- With population, agents averaged 16 genes within 50 generations and a group of 4 has a 97% chance of being fit. This shows the evolutionary plateau because individuals don't feel the pressure to evolve further when their groups are always fit [3]
- After adding an individual incentive to fit people within a fit group, the individuals in the population goes up to 20 genes with a 1 value [3]
- Takeaway is that cooperation helps in situations where there is no incentive to achieve a partial trait, but an individual incentive is needed to achieve the complete trait even in a cooperative setting [3]
