**1. Hinton & Nowlan (1987), _How Learning Can Guide Evolution_.** Learning changes the shape of the search space evolution works in, giving it good paths toward sets of co-adapted alleles, even though what an organism learns is never passed back to its genes. The paper demonstrates this with a simulation in which learning organisms evolve far faster than non-learning ones. Learning is just random guessing, but it is enough to turn a needle-in-a-haystack search into a gradual one.  
_Thesis relevance:_ This is where the mechanism your thesis builds on comes from. Its argument that Lamarckian inheritance would require inverting the genotype-to-phenotype map is also worth revisiting for your Baldwin-vs-Lamarck comparison. [toronto](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/absps/evolution.htm)

**2. Turney, _Myths and Legends of the Baldwin Effect_.** This position paper argues that the Baldwin effect is widely misunderstood in evolutionary computation: it concerns both the costs and benefits of lifetime learning, it is not Lamarckian, and a Lamarckian algorithm is not generally better than a Baldwinian one.
_Thesis relevance:_ It gives you precise terminology and a caution against treating the Baldwin effect as only "learning helps evolution." Fernando et al. cite it directly. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0212036)

**3. Houghton, _Cooperation as well as learning_.** This commentary reuses the Hinton–Nowlan simulation but replaces learning with cooperation, showing that cooperation can also help evolution discover complex multi-gene traits much faster. The author describes the simulation as a demonstration rather than a proof, and notes it applies best to behavioral and cognitive traits.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It's a side paper. It shows the Baldwin mechanism is really about bridging a gap where partial solutions are otherwise worthless, which helps when you discuss when the mechanism should or shouldn't transfer to other settings. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.15609)

**4. Fernando et al. (2018), _Meta-Learning by the Baldwin Effect_.** The paper shows the Baldwin effect can evolve few-shot supervised and reinforcement learning by shaping the initial parameters and hyperparameters of deep learning algorithms, reaching results comparable to MAML without needing gradients backpropagated to those parameters. It tests this on sinusoid regression and two cheetah RL tasks. It also compares Baldwinian, Lamarckian, and Darwinian evolution, and finds Baldwinian works better when the task distribution is broad or changing, while Lamarckian works better when it is narrow.  
_Thesis relevance:_ This is your base paper. The sinusoid regression experiment is the natural place to try harder function families, and the Baldwin-vs-Lamarck result suggests task-distribution breadth is a variable worth testing directly. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.07917)

**5. Finn, Abbeel & Levine (2017), _MAML_.** MAML trains a model's initial parameters so that a small number of gradient steps on a new task produces good performance, and it works across classification, regression, and reinforcement learning. Its regression benchmark is sinusoid fitting with varying amplitude and phase, which is the same problem Fernando et al. use.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It is your main baseline. MAML also reports that dropping second derivatives barely hurt performance on one benchmark, which is relevant because Fernando et al. present avoiding second-order gradients as an advantage of the evolutionary approach. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03400)

**6. Wong et al., _Evolutionary Optimization of PINNs (Baldwinian-PINNs)_.** The authors evolve physics-informed networks using an outer evolutionary loop over a distribution of physics tasks and an inner lifetime-learning step, and report more than an order of magnitude better accuracy than gradient-based meta-learning at a fraction of the computation. Only the evolved parameters are inherited, not the output weights learned during a lifetime, so it is Baldwinian rather than Lamarckian.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It shows a Baldwinian approach beating MAML-style methods in another domain, largely because its inner learning step is cheap and exact. That design idea could carry over to a regression setting like sinusoids. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03243)

**7. Soltoggio, Stanley & Risi, _Born to Learn_.** This survey covers evolved plastic artificial neural networks, meaning networks whose design is partly determined by evolution and which also change and learn in response to experience. It reviews evolved learning rules, architectures, and neuromodulation, and includes a discussion of the Baldwin effect in static versus changing environments.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It's most useful for related work and vocabulary, since it places Fernando et al. within the broader field of evolving the ability to learn. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10371)

**8. Najarro & Risi, _Meta-Learning through Hebbian Plasticity in Random Networks_.** Instead of optimizing network weights directly, this work searches for synapse-specific Hebbian learning rules that let a randomly initialized network organize its own weights during its lifetime, and this let a simulated quadruped adapt to leg damage not seen during training. The rules are found with an evolution strategy, and no reward signal is given during the agent's lifetime.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It offers a different answer to "what should evolution encode?" Here it is the learning rule rather than the initial weights, which could be a direction for extending Fernando et al. [neurips](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/ee23e7ad9b473ad072d57aaa9b2a5222-Paper.pdf)

**9. Gupta et al. (2021), _Embodied Intelligence via Learning and Evolution_.** The authors introduce a framework that evolves agent morphologies while each learns control with reinforcement learning, and they report a morphological Baldwin effect in which evolution selects bodies that learn faster. Each new agent learns from scratch, so only morphology is inherited, and there was no explicit selection pressure for learning speed.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It's evidence that the Baldwin effect shows up at large scale in a much richer setting than regression, and it is purely Baldwinian in structure. [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25874-z)

**10. Luo et al., _Lamarck's Revenge_.** The authors compare a Lamarckian system, where learned parts of a robot's brain are inherited, with a Darwinian one where they are not, and find Lamarckism amplifies "morphological intelligence" because newborn robots inherit brains that match their bodies better. I've only seen the abstract and start of the introduction.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It is the counterpoint to Turney and to Fernando et al.'s finding on broad task distributions, and together they frame your Baldwin-vs-Lamarck question. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.13099)

**11. Salimans et al., _Evolution Strategies as a Scalable Alternative to RL_.** The paper shows that evolution strategies, a class of black-box optimizers, can be competitive with standard RL on MuJoCo and Atari, and that they scale extremely well with the number of CPUs available. The authors also name meta-learning as a future application.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It's a candidate outer-loop optimizer for evolutionary meta-learning and supports the argument that evolution can scale as an alternative to gradient-based meta-learning. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03864)

**12. Fontanari & Santos, _The Revival of the Baldwin Effect_.** The authors argue that Hinton and Nowlan actually showed that learning ability is easily selected for, and then demonstrate the Baldwin effect proper by showing that learning greatly raises the probability that the learned behavior becomes innate. I've only seen the abstract and start of the introduction.  
_Thesis relevance:_ It separates "learning is selected for" from "learning becomes innate," a distinction worth defining carefully for meta-learning, where evolution may only be shaping learning biases rather than fixing behaviors. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.08411)
